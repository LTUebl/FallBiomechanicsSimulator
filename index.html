Empirical & Computational Analysis Across 300 Discrete Fall Heights and Rotational Regimes
Regime I · Localized Extremity Trauma 1.0 m – 3.0 m

Dominated by peripheral extremity fractures (distal radius Colles', bimalleolar ankle, patellar). High survival rate (>98%). Somatic kinetic energy remains below 2,500 J. Head trauma occurs primarily if neuromuscular bracing reflex fails or during unbraced backward occipital impact.

Impact Velocity: 4.4 – 7.7 m/s · Mean GRF: 4.5 – 14.0 kN · Mortality: < 1.5%
Regime II · Axial Deceleration & Bimodal Bifurcation 3.1 m – 12.0 m

Marked by dramatic bimodal distribution dictated by mid-air rotational dynamics. Feet-first landings transmit severe compressive shock through the axial skeleton (calcanei → tibiae → pelvic ring → thoracolumbar spine burst fractures). Head-first/torso landings result in severe craniocerebral destruction and high fatality.

Impact Velocity: 7.8 – 15.3 m/s · Mean GRF: 15.0 – 48.0 kN · Mortality: 15% – 50%
Regime III · Catastrophic Polytrauma & Visceral Rupture 12.1 m – 30.0 m+

Kinetic energy exceeds 12,000 J with impact velocity approaching terminal aerodynamic values (>20 m/s). Generalized comminuted skeletal fragmentation, traumatic aortic transection, hepatic/splenic avulsion, and basilar ring fractures. Landing orientation alters primary fracture locus but lethality approaches 90–100% (LD₅₀ ≈ 12–15 m).

Impact Velocity: 15.4 – 24.2 m/s · Mean GRF: > 50 kN · Mortality: > 85% – 99%

Forensic Anthropology & Biomechanics Commentary on Bimodal Impact Dynamics: As established by Henriques et al. (2023) and Cartales et al. (2024), human falls from intermediate heights (3–12m) display a marked bimodal trauma pattern. When angular velocity is low (e.g. feet-first jump or slip), ground reaction force is attenuated through the lower extremities via ankle dorsiflexion, knee flexion, and calcaneal crushing before reaching the spinal axis. Conversely, when initial angular momentum or tripping induces torso rotation, the victim impacts in a horizontal or inverted attitude, circumventing lower limb compliance and directly delivering high deceleration energy to the skull and visceral cavity.

Biomechanical Theory, Multi-Body Dynamics & Forensic References

1. Articulated Multibody Dynamics (MBD) Formulation

The simulator implements a planar 10-segment articulated human body model (head/neck, thorax, abdomen/pelvis, bilateral thighs, bilateral shanks, bilateral arms). The generalized equations of motion are expressed in Lagrange form:

$$ \mathbf{M}(\mathbf{q}) \ddot{\mathbf{q}} + \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{q}, \dot{\mathbf{q}})\dot{\mathbf{q}} + \mathbf{G}(\mathbf{q}) = \mathbf{\tau}_{\text{joint}} + \mathbf{J}^T \mathbf{F}_{\text{contact}} $$

where $\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{q})$ is the mass-inertia matrix, $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{q},\dot{\mathbf{q}})$ represents Coriolis and centrifugal terms, $\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{q})$ is the gravitational generalized force vector, $\mathbf{\tau}_{\text{joint}}$ models passive viscoelastic joint stiffness and active neuromuscular bracing torques, and $\mathbf{J}^T \mathbf{F}_{\text{contact}}$ maps substrate contact forces to generalized coordinates. Numerical integration is performed using a 4th-order Runge-Kutta (RK4) algorithm with adaptive sub-stepping ($\Delta t = 0.1\,\text{ms}$) during contact penetration.

2. Viscoelastic Contact Kinetics (Hunt-Crossley Model)

To avoid unphysical instantaneous velocity jumps and negative contact forces during restitution, normal ground reaction forces $F_n$ are computed via the non-linear Hunt-Crossley formulation:

$$ F_n(\delta, \dot{\delta}) = k_{\text{surf}} \, \delta^n + \lambda \, \delta^n \, \dot{\delta} \quad (\text{for } \delta > 0, \ F_n \ge 0) $$

where $\delta$ is the anatomical segment penetration depth into the substrate, $\dot{\delta}$ is penetration velocity, $n \approx 1.5$ corresponds to Hertzian contact geometry, and $\lambda = \frac{3(1-e)k_{\text{surf}}}{2 e v_{\text{impact}}}$ represents the non-linear hysteretic damping factor calibrated to the coefficient of restitution $e$. Tangential friction $F_t$ is modeled via regularized Coulomb friction $F_t = \mu \, F_n \tanh(v_t / v_{\text{slip}})$.

3. Skeletal Fracture Thresholds & Tissue Tolerance Scaling

Fracture risk is evaluated using localized peak stress $\sigma$ relative to anatomical yield thresholds $\sigma_y$. Tissue degradation or reinforcement is governed by the Bone Mineral Density (BMD) tolerance scale factor $T$:

$$ F_{\text{crit, adjusted}} = T \cdot F_{\text{crit, baseline}}, \quad \text{Injury Ratio } R = \frac{F_{\text{peak}}}{F_{\text{crit, adjusted}}} $$
Cranium / Frontal Bone F_crit = 4.50 kN

Linear cranial fracture limit: 4.5 kN; Depressed/comminuted: >7.0 kN. Head Injury Criterion threshold $\text{HIC}_{15} \ge 1000$ indicates 50% probability of severe neurotrauma (AIS 4+).

Cervical Spine (C1–C7) F_crit = 4.00 kN

Axial compression tolerance: 4.0 kN; Combined flexion-compression failure: 2.8 kN·m. High risk of Jefferson or Hangman fractures during inverted impacts.

Thoracolumbar Spine (T12–L3) F_crit = 6.50 kN

Vertebral body compression/burst limit: 6.5 kN. Typical in vertical axial decelerations where energy propagates from calcanei through extended lower limbs.

Pelvic Ring & Acetabulum F_crit = 8.00 kN

Pelvic fracture limit: 8.0 kN lateral compression; 10.0 kN vertical shear. High correlation with retroperitoneal hemorrhage and severe vascular injury.

Femur (Diaphysis & Neck) F_crit = 10.00 kN

Femoral shaft axial compressive yield: 10.0 kN; 3-point bending limit: 350 N·m. In elderly cohorts ($T = 0.40$), femoral neck fracture threshold drops to 2.8 kN.

Tibia, Fibula & Calcaneus F_crit = 7.00 kN

Tibial pilon fracture: 7.0 kN; Calcaneal crush (Don Juan fracture): 5.0 kN. Primary energy absorption structure in upright feet-first landing attitudes.
